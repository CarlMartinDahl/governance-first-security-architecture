# Governance-First Security Architecture - Governance Decision Assessment Workshop Offer v0.1

## Status

Commercial validation package.

This is not a new model layer.

This is not implementation authorization.

This is not prototype approval.

This is not a security claim.

This is not a compliance claim.

This is a narrow workshop/assessment offer for testing whether the Governance-First Security Architecture addresses a real paid problem.

## Purpose

The purpose of this offer is to validate whether organizations will pay for help answering one concrete governance question:

```text
Which AI/security decisions can be allowed, blocked, escalated, exported, and audited?
```

The purpose is not to sell software.

The purpose is not to prove product-market fit.

The purpose is not to position the model as a security platform.

## Recommended Positioning

Use this positioning:

```text
Governance Decision Assessment for sensitive AI-assisted decisions.
```

Avoid this positioning:

```text
New security architecture.
Security platform.
AI security agent.
Compliance platform.
Runtime enforcement system.
```

## Target Customers

Primary target customers:

- regulated companies using or evaluating AI-assisted workflows,
- enterprise SaaS companies selling to regulated or security-conscious customers,
- companies facing board, customer, legal, risk, or security questions about AI use,
- teams that need to explain how sensitive AI-assisted decisions are controlled and reviewed.

Primary buyer or sponsor candidates:

- CTO,
- CISO,
- Head of Risk,
- AI Governance Lead,
- Legal / Compliance Lead,
- Product or Platform Owner responsible for enterprise readiness.

## Pain Statement

Organizations are adopting AI faster than their governance processes can explain.

The pain is not only:

```text
Can we use AI?
```

The sharper pain is:

```text
When AI is involved in a sensitive workflow, who is allowed to decide,
what evidence is required,
what must be blocked,
what must be escalated,
what may leave the organization,
and what audit record is needed afterward?
```

Best for:

Suggested participants:

- product or process owner,
- technical owner,

## Workshop Scope

In scope:

- one selected AI-assisted or security-adjacent decision workflow,
- decision inventory,
- authority mapping,
- allow/block/escalate review,
- evidence requirement review,
- egress/export review,
- stop-state gap review,
- human/AI accountability review,
- auditability review,
- do-not-automate identification,
- summary of governance gaps and next safe actions.

Out of scope:

- software implementation,
- runtime enforcement,
- live integrations,
- real-time monitoring,
- active scanning,
- remediation,
- security-agent behavior,
- compliance certification,
- legal advice,
- processing real sensitive data,
- production readiness claims.

## Input Needed From Customer

The customer should bring:

- a description of one AI-assisted or AI-adjacent workflow,
- example decision types,
- relevant policy excerpts if available,
- known approval or escalation rules,
- known data export or data-sharing concerns,
- current audit or evidence expectations,
- known customer, board, legal, risk, or security questions.

The workshop should avoid:

- real secrets,
- production credentials,
- live customer personal data,
- sensitive incident details unless already approved for review,
- any material the customer is not authorized to share.

## Assessment Method

The assessment uses the Governance-First Security Architecture as a review lens.

The core review path is:

```text
1. Identify sensitive decision.
2. Identify actor and authority.
3. Identify AI involvement.
4. Identify required evidence.
5. Classify allow/block/escalate outcome.
6. Identify what may leave the system or organization.
7. Identify required human review.
8. Identify stop states.
9. Identify audit record requirements.
10. Identify what should not be automated.
```

## Core Assessment Questions

Ask:

1. What decision is being made?
2. Who or what initiates the decision?
3. Is AI recommending, drafting, classifying, approving, or acting?
4. Who has authority to approve the decision?
5. What evidence is required before the decision is allowed?
6. What evidence is insufficient or stale?
7. What must be blocked automatically?
8. What must be escalated to human review?
9. What data or result may leave the workflow?
10. What data or result must not leave the workflow?
11. What audit record would be needed later?
12. What would be unsafe to automate?

## Deliverables

Recommended deliverables:

1. Governance decision map.
2. Allow/block/escalate/export/audit matrix.
3. Stop-state gap list.
4. Evidence requirement summary.
5. Human/AI accountability gap list.
6. Do-not-automate list.
7. Short executive summary for board, customer, legal, risk, or security discussion.
8. Recommended next safe review step.

## Success Criteria

The workshop is successful if:

- the customer can name the sensitive decisions that need governance,
- ambiguous authority is reduced,
- evidence requirements become clearer,
- stop and escalation conditions become visible,
- egress/export concerns are identified,
- audit needs become concrete,
- unsafe automation candidates are identified,
- the customer can decide whether deeper review is worth paying for.

## Commercial Validation Criteria

This offer supports product-market validation only if:

- at least 2-3 organizations are willing to pay for the workshop or assessment,
- the same pain appears repeatedly across customers,
- buyers can identify budget ownership,
- the output is useful without software,
- customers request repeatable templates, assessment reports, or a synthetic simulator only after the assessment proves useful.

Positive comments are not product-market fit.

Unpaid interest is not product-market fit.

Investor excitement is not product-market fit.

## Pricing Hypothesis

Initial pricing should be tested, not assumed.

Possible starting points:

```text
Short Assessment: fixed low-friction fee.
Full-Day Assessment: higher fixed fee with written output.
Follow-up Advisory: scoped retainer only if the customer asks for continued support.
```

Do not present pricing as validated.

Do not discount to zero if the goal is to test willingness to pay.

## Current Decision

The current safe commercial step is:

```text
Run 2-3 tightly scoped paid workshops or assessments before any software build, product-company positioning, or prototype implementation discussion.
```

If customers pay for this assessment and repeat the same pain, the next step may be a more structured service package.

If customers only say the idea is interesting, the model should remain a consulting/review framework.

If customers ask for software, the request must still pass the documentation freeze gate, provider/platform constraints, prototype boundary review, and post-review revision process before any build discussion.
